DoomSquirrel Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 17, 2026

DoomSquirrel is a game by Second Pass LLC ("Second Pass," "we," "us"). You memorize a picture, scroll a feed, and tap the match as fast as you can. We built it to be fun first, so we keep what we collect small. This policy explains what the game collects, why, who helps us run it, and the choices you have. It adds detail to our company privacy policy; for DoomSquirrel, this page is the one that applies. Your use of the game is also governed by our Terms of Service.

The short version

  • You don't need to create an account or give us your email to play.
  • Your stars, best times and settings stay on your phone.
  • If you play friend challenges, we store the name you pick and your times so friends can see the leaderboard.
  • The game shows ads from Google AdMob, which collects information about your device to show and measure ads. You decide whether ads can be personalized.
  • Purchases go through Apple, which handles your payment details; we don't receive them.
  • We don't sell your personal information for money. Some state laws treat sharing data with ad partners for personalized ads as a "sale" or "sharing," and you can opt out of that, as explained below.

What stays on your phone

Your star balance, best times, chosen level and picture pack, packs you unlocked with stars, and the name you chose are saved on your device rather than on our servers. Deleting the app deletes them from your device.

What we collect, and why

A random player ID. When you first open the game, it signs you in anonymously using Google Firebase. This creates a random ID that isn't linked to your name, email or phone number. We use it so the game can tell your challenge times and votes apart from everyone else's.

Friend challenges. If you create or join a challenge, we store the name you chose (up to 16 characters), the challenge code, the level and picture pack, and your result: your time, wrong taps, stars, and when you started and finished. Anyone with the challenge code can see the names, times and stars on that leaderboard. Please don't use your full name or anything private as your player name.

Name reports. If you report another player's name as inappropriate, we store your random player ID, the reported player's ID and name, the challenge code, and when you sent it, so we can review the report and rename or remove the name. Player names are also checked against a filter for offensive words on your device before they're used.

Pack votes. If you vote for a future picture pack, we store which packs you voted for, linked to your random player ID, so we can count votes and you can change yours.

Technical information. Like any online service, Firebase receives your IP address and basic device information when the game connects to it, to deliver the service and keep it secure.

Purchases. If you buy a picture pack, Apple processes the payment. Our purchase service, RevenueCat, receives a record of what you bought, your Apple receipt, and a random ID so it can unlock the pack and restore it later. We don't receive your card or bank details.

Advertising. DoomSquirrel shows banner ads on menus, a full-screen ad every few rounds, and optional video ads you can watch for bonus stars. These come from Google AdMob. Google may collect your device's advertising identifier (if you allow tracking; otherwise your iPhone withholds it), your IP address and the approximate location it implies, your device type and settings, and how you interact with ads. Google uses this to show ads, limit how often you see the same one, measure results and prevent fraud. DoomSquirrel doesn't ask for access to your device's location (GPS), so the game doesn't provide your precise location to us or our ad partners. Google and its ad partners may estimate your approximate location (such as your city or region) from your IP address. If you're shown Google's consent message, any choice it offers about precise geolocation applies to data partners may have from other sources, not from DoomSquirrel. How Google handles this data is described at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Your choices about ads

  • Tracking. On iPhone, the game asks whether it may track your activity across other companies' apps and websites. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, ads aren't personalized using your advertising identifier. You can change this anytime in Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Tracking.
  • Consent messages. If you're in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, or a US state with its own privacy law, the game shows Google's privacy message the first time you open it. It lets you choose how your data is used for ads, or opt out of the sale or sharing of your information for personalized advertising.
  • Changing your choice. Where your region requires it, a Privacy choices link on the game's home screen reopens Google's message so you can change your choice at any time.
  • You can play the game with non-personalized ads.

Who helps us run DoomSquirrel

We share information with the companies that run parts of the game for us, for that purpose:

  • Google Firebase for anonymous sign-in and storing challenge results and votes.
  • Google AdMob for ads, as described above.
  • RevenueCat for confirming and restoring purchases.
  • Apple for payments, app delivery, and crash reports if you've chosen to share them with developers.

Other players can see your player name, times and stars on the leaderboard of any challenge you join. We may also share information if the law requires it, to protect the rights and safety of players, Second Pass, or others, to prevent fraud or abuse, or as part of a merger, acquisition, or sale of all or part of our business, subject to this policy.

How long we keep it

We keep challenge results and votes while DoomSquirrel is running, so leaderboards keep working, unless you ask us to delete them sooner. We keep name reports as long as needed to review them and prevent abuse. Purchase records are kept as long as needed to restore purchases and meet tax and accounting rules. Information on your phone is removed when you delete the app.

Your rights

You can ask us what we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. Because you play without an account, we can't look you up by name. When you write to us, include the challenge codes you played and the player name you used, and we'll find your results and remove them. We may ask for details like these to confirm the request is yours. Depending on where you live, you may have other rights under local law, such as opting out of targeted advertising; we'll honor them as the law requires, and using them won't affect how we treat you, except as the law allows. Write to hello@secondpass.me.

Children

DoomSquirrel is made for a general audience and isn't directed to children under 13. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has shared information with us, contact us and we'll delete it.

Keeping it safe

Data sent from the game is encrypted on its way to our service providers, and our database rules limit each player to changing only their own results and votes. We use reasonable measures to protect your information, but no system is completely secure, so we can't guarantee absolute security.

Where it's stored

Second Pass is based in the United States, and our service providers may store and process information in the United States and other countries.

Changes to this policy

If we change how DoomSquirrel uses your information, we'll update this page and the date at the top. For important changes, we'll also let you know in the game.

Contact

Questions about your privacy? Email us at hello@secondpass.me and we'll get back to you.
